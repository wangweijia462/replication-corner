## 1. Central Contribution Statement

This paper conceptually replicates Jo and Li (2026)—who find that AI-assisted writing attenuates the institutional reputation premium in SSRN marketing working-paper diffusion—by testing whether the same moderating pattern holds in a structurally distinct context: 480 peer-reviewed Chinese CNKI journal articles on business and management topics published between 2023 and 2026. Using an abstract-level stylometric AI-style score as a proxy for AI-like writing and researcher-coded institutional prestige rankings, we find that institutional prestige and AI-style writing each independently predict greater downloads and citations. However, the interaction between AI-style writing and institutional prestige is consistently small, signed negative in most specifications, and statistically insignificant across all four combinations of outcome variable and prestige operationalization. These results establish peer-reviewed journal publication context—characterized by editorial gatekeeping, peer review, and mandatory institutional disclosure—as an important boundary condition: the reputation-equalizing potential of AI-style writing documented for the preprint ecosystem does not extend robustly to the Chinese academic journal market, where prestige signals remain intact and effective.

## 2. Abstract (120–150 words)

Jo and Li (2026) report that AI-assisted writing attenuates the institutional reputation premium in knowledge diffusion among SSRN marketing working papers. We conceptually replicate this finding using 480 peer-reviewed journal articles on business and management topics published in CNKI (2023–2026). We measure AI involvement via an abstract-level stylometric proxy for AI-like writing patterns and institutional prestige via a researcher-coded, four-tier ranking. Consistent with the original study, institutional prestige (β = 0.52 for downloads, β = 0.24 for citations) and AI-style score (β = 0.27 and β = 0.18, respectively) each independently predict greater dissemination. However, the reputation × AI-style interaction is consistently near zero and statistically insignificant across all specifications. This boundary-condition finding suggests that peer-reviewed publication contexts—where editorial filtering preserves prestige signals—may limit AI's equalizing potential for knowledge diffusion.

4. **Paragraph 4 — Implications for the AI democratization thesis (≈110 words).** Engage carefully with the broader claim that AI democratizes academic knowledge production. The finding suggests AI's equalizing potential may be context-dependent: it may be stronger in low-gatekeeping, open-access contexts (SSRN) than in formal publication venues with established quality signals. This has implications for platforms, journal editors, and researchers considering whether AI-assisted writing is a leveling tool or merely a style convention adopted differentially across institutional tiers. Notably, even the CNKI data show that high-prestige institutions produce slightly HIGHER AI-style scores on average (mean 0.250 vs. 0.268 for low-prestige), which weakens the "AI-equalizes style" premise in the first place.

$$
\log(Y_i + 1) = \alpha + \beta_1 \cdot \text{Prestige\_High}_i + \beta_2 \cdot \text{AI\_z}_i + \beta_3 \cdot (\text{Prestige\_High}_i \times \text{AI\_z}_i) + \beta_4 \cdot \log(\text{Age}_i) + \beta_5 \cdot \text{AuthorCount}_i + \beta_6 \cdot \log(\text{AbsLength}_i) + \sum_{t=2024}^{2026} \delta_t \cdot \text{Year}_t + \varepsilon_i
$$

### Equation 2 — Continuous Prestige-Score Operationalization

$$
\log(Y_i + 1) = \alpha + \beta_1 \cdot \text{PrestigeScore\_z}_i + \beta_2 \cdot \text{AI\_z}_i + \beta_3 \cdot (\text{PrestigeScore\_z}_i \times \text{AI\_z}_i) + \beta_4 \cdot \log(\text{Age}_i) + \beta_5 \cdot \text{AuthorCount}_i + \beta_6 \cdot \log(\text{AbsLength}_i) + \sum_{t=2024}^{2026} \delta_t \cdot \text{Year}_t + \varepsilon_i
$$

**Where:**
- $Y_i \in \{\text{downloads}_i,\ \text{citations}_i\}$
- $\text{Prestige\_High}_i \in \{0, 1\}$: binary indicator, 1 if any author is affiliated with a 985/C9/211/DFC-ranked institution
- $\text{PrestigeScore\_z}_i$: standardized four-level prestige score (1 = non-ranked, 2 = 211/DFC, 3 = 985, 4 = C9); standardized to mean 0, SD 1
- $\text{AI\_z}_i$: abstract-level AI-style score standardized to mean 0, SD 1
- $\text{Age}_i$: days between publication date and data-collection date (log-transformed)
- $\text{AuthorCount}_i$: number of listed authors
- $\text{AbsLength}_i$: abstract word/character count (log-transformed)
- $\text{Year}_t$: year dummies (2023 = reference category)
- $\varepsilon_i$: error term; HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors throughout
- $\beta_3$ is the coefficient of central inferential interest

**Reporting convention:** Report β, HC3 SE, z-statistic, p-value, and 95% CI for every coefficient in Table 2. Emphasize the CI width for β₃ to show that the interval encompasses meaningful positive and negative values, demonstrating that the data cannot distinguish the effect from zero in either direction.

## 9. Limitations to Openly Acknowledge

Write these limitations explicitly, in Section 5 (Discussion) or at the start of Section 6 (Conclusion):

1. **Proxy measurement of AI involvement.** The ai_style_score is an abstract-level stylometric proxy. It captures formulaic writing patterns associated with AI assistance but does not confirm actual AI use. Authors may write in a structured, templated style without any AI tool, and AI-assisted papers may not always exhibit the specific patterns captured.

2. **Observational design, no causal identification.** OLS regressions cannot establish that prestige *causes* downloads or that AI-style *causes* citation uptake. Reverse causality (popular papers are cited more and may also attract authors who use AI tools) and omitted-variable bias (top institutions have more resources, better networks, and are cited for reasons unrelated to writing style) are not fully ruled out.

3. **Platform-specific download mechanics.** CNKI download counts reflect platform-level behavior including crawler traffic, auto-downloads, and librarian bulk-access patterns, not only deliberate scholarly reading. They may not map cleanly to intellectual diffusion as measured on SSRN.

4. **Short citation window and left-censoring.** With many articles published in 2025–2026, citation counts are severely truncated. Citation-based results should be interpreted cautiously, as the full citation trajectory of recent articles is unobserved.

5. **Non-representative sampling of Chinese academic output.** The sample covers only business/management topics on AI and related themes, drawn from a convenience sample of CNKI articles. The findings may not generalize to other disciplinary domains, to Chinese social science broadly, or to STEM fields.

6. **No controls for individual author reputation.** Author h-index, prior publication count, and professional network centrality are correlated with both institutional prestige and download counts but are not included in the model. Omitting these controls may inflate the prestige coefficient and partially confound the interaction estimate.

7. **Prestige coding is researcher-imposed and ordinal.** The four-tier classification is not a validated continuous measure. Converting it to a z-score assumes equal interval distances across tiers, which may not hold in practice.

8. **Temporal confounds.** The sample spans 2023–2026, a period of rapid generative AI diffusion. Year fixed effects capture some but not all of the secular increase in AI-style writing. Within-year heterogeneity in AI tool adoption may create residual confounding not captured by annual dummies.

9. **Cross-context comparability with Jo and Li (2026).** The original study uses SSRN preprints (mostly working papers), while this study uses published journal articles. The two populations differ on quality distribution, topic scope, author stage (faculty vs. mixed), and platform incentives. The comparison is conceptual, not methodologically exact.

10. **Topic heterogeneity within the sample.** Articles cover a wide range of themes (AI governance, supply chain, ESG, organizational behavior, corporate finance). Topic interest may independently drive downloads; without topic fixed effects in the main models, some heterogeneity is absorbed into the error term.
